## Which customers are worth building for?

One prospect wants a different application. A partner wants exclusivity. Both could lead to business, but each asks for different product work, sales effort and delivery capacity.

### Choose where to focus

I compare the strongest options: what customers have shown us, how urgently they need a solution, whether we can reach decision-makers, what a contract could be worth and what it would take to deliver.

## Who will lead the sale after the first meeting?

The CEO is interested. Procurement, IT and operations have different requirements. You are still the person coordinating them all.

I take responsibility for the opportunity we define together. I map the buying process, shape the initial project with your team and lead the commercial discussions, proposal and negotiation work.

## A pilot with interest, but no buying decision

### An illustrative opportunity review

Fictional example, not a client result.

### The situation

A robotics pilot has met its technical targets. The sponsor wants pricing for several sites. The startup has not met the budget owner or confirmed who can approve deployment.

### What is missing

Technical interest is clear. Funding, purchasing authority and deployment ownership are not. A detailed multi-site proposal would depend on assumptions the customer has not confirmed.

### Questions to answer

Who owns the cost the deployment is meant to reduce? Which budget could fund it? What evidence does that person need? Who will take responsibility for site readiness, integration and ongoing operation?

### What I would do

Work with the sponsor to bring the budget owner into the discussion. Build the business case around the customer’s baseline, expected benefits, deployment costs and operational responsibilities. Keep measured pilot findings separate from assumptions.

Shape an initial paid deployment with the technical and delivery leads. Confirm the procurement, IT, safety and contractual reviews before relying on a closing date.

### What the founder decides

Approve the scope, acceptable commercial terms, delivery capacity and limit on further unpaid work.

### What progress would look like

The budget owner joins the discussion, a funding route is identified and the customer confirms the approval steps. If those are still missing, reconsider further technical work before preparing the wider rollout proposal.
