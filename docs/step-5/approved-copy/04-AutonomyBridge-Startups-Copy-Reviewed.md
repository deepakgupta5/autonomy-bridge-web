## What the work can look like

### Illustrative commercial opportunity review extract

Fictional scenario demonstrating an approach. This is not a client engagement or an achieved result.

### The situation

A robotics pilot met its technical targets. The operational sponsor has requested pricing for several sites. The startup has not met the budget owner, and nobody has confirmed who would approve deployment.

### My assessment

The customer has demonstrated technical interest. Funding, purchasing authority and deployment ownership remain unresolved. Preparing a detailed multi-site proposal would commit the startup to assumptions the customer has not agreed.

### Questions to resolve

Who owns the cost the deployment is intended to reduce? Which budget could fund it? What evidence does that person need? Who accepts responsibility for site readiness, integration and ongoing operation?

### What I would lead

Arrange a discussion with the sponsor and budget owner. Build the commercial case around the customer’s agreed baseline, expected benefit, deployment costs and operational responsibilities. Separate measured pilot findings from assumptions that still need testing.

With the technical and delivery leads, shape an initial paid deployment. Confirm the customer’s purchasing sequence, including any procurement, IT, safety and contractual reviews, before treating a proposed closing date as credible.

### Decision requested from the founder

Approve the proposed scope, acceptable commercial terms, delivery capacity and limit on further unpaid work.

### Evidence of progress

The budget owner participates, the funding route is identified and the customer confirms the steps required for approval. If those remain unresolved, reconsider further technical effort before preparing the wider rollout proposal.
